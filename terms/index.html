The game, played safely

STREETDARE is a game to help strangers meet. Every dare is optional and depends on both people wanting to do it.

  • Consent first. Only do a dare if the other person, and anyone else involved, is happy to. A "no" ends it, no questions asked.
  • Use your judgement. Skip any dare that could hurt someone, break a law, a venue's rules or someone's property, or that doesn't feel right in the moment. You can always spin again.
  • Respect people around you. Don't photograph or film anyone who hasn't agreed to it.
  • Drinks are never required. Some dares mention a drink. They're marked 18+, water always counts, and nobody has to drink. Don't push anyone to.
  • You're responsible for what you do. STREETDARE suggests dares; it doesn't make you do them. As far as the law allows, we aren't liable for what happens when you choose to do one.

Play at your own risk

STREETDARE is played in real life, often at night, in crowded places and sometimes after a few drinks. You take part at your own risk and you're responsible for your own choices, safety and belongings.

  • Your phone and your things. Some dares involve phones, photos or apps. Only hand your phone to someone if you're comfortable with it, stay next to it, and lock it or close private apps first. We aren't responsible for lost, stolen or damaged phones, or for what someone sees or does on them.
  • Look after each other. Know your limits, keep an eye on your friends, and stop if anyone is too drunk to really say yes.
  • Report problems. If someone makes you feel unsafe while playing, say no, walk away and tell venue staff. You can also report them to us on the contact page (Safety → Report someone). In an emergency, call 112.

Fair play: reports and warnings

Be the reason someone's night got better, never the reason it got worse. STREETDARE is about meeting people, so we expect everyone who plays to respect others and their boundaries. Anyone can skip any dare, and nobody should be pushed into something they wouldn't want to do, whether they've been drinking or not.

If someone reports you through our contact page, this is what happens:

  • First report: we send you a private warning by email, telling you what was reported.
  • Second report: we switch off your shirt's QR code (scanning it shows that the shirt is switched off) and/or block your account from joining Shirtless Pass groups.
  • Serious reports (violence, sexual harassment or anything illegal): we switch off your shirt and/or block your account straight away, without a warning first. We may also share information with the police if they ask for it and the law requires it.
  • We never tell the person you reported who reported them, and we never name anyone in public.
  • We don't investigate crimes ourselves. If something serious happens, tell the venue staff and contact the police (112 in Spain and Hungary).
  • If you think we made a mistake, write to us on the contact page and we'll look at it again.
